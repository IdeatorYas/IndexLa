# 2. The Problem

Modern investing is fragmented.

Assets exist across different chains, wallets, protocols, exchanges, and financial platforms. Building and managing a diversified portfolio can require multiple transactions, interfaces, bridges, approvals, and execution venues.

This creates three connected problems.

### Fragmented Investing

Multi-asset and multi-chain investing can require users to manage:

- Separate wallets and positions
- Multiple swaps and bridges
- Different execution venues
- Manual portfolio rebalancing
- Fragmented portfolio visibility

A diversified portfolio becomes a collection of individual transactions rather than one coherent portfolio.

### Manual Strategy Execution

Investors may know exactly what they want to do but still have to execute manually.

DCA, rebalancing, take profit, stop loss, Fear & Greed, RSI, and momentum strategies require consistent action when predefined conditions occur.

The problem is not a lack of strategies.

The problem is turning defined strategies into consistent, programmable execution.

### Creators Lack Portfolio Infrastructure

Financial creators can build large audiences around research, market views, and investment strategies.

Yet their thesis often remains content.

Creators need infrastructure to:

- Turn a thesis into an investable portfolio
- Allow followers to discover and follow portfolios
- Automate defined strategies
- Build multiple portfolios and indexes
- Earn recurring revenue from portfolio activity
- Monetize strategy access
- Receive direct support from their community

INDEXLA connects financial influence with portfolio infrastructure.

# 6. Why the Architecture Gap Is Structural

Existing portfolio platforms can be built around vault or pool-based models where assets are deposited into collective smart contracts.

INDEXLA is built around individual asset ownership with scoped execution permissions.

### 01 — Individual Asset Ownership

Vault models rely on users depositing assets into a shared contract.

INDEXLA is designed so users retain ownership of their underlying assets.

Adding this model to a vault architecture is not simply a front-end feature change. It changes the underlying custody and execution assumptions of the platform.

### 02 — Scoped Non-Custodial Permissions

INDEXLA can authorize specific execution actions without taking ownership of user assets.

Vault models hold the assets; they cannot grant "execute but never withdraw" permissions because custody is the entire premise.

### 03 — Execution-Level Creator Economics

INDEXLA routes creator economics directly from applicable portfolio execution activity.

Vault architectures pool fees to the protocol or liquidity providers collectively; retrofitting per-creator, per-portfolio splits at execution would require rebuilding the core fee-routing layer.

A competitor could build an INDEXLA-like product.

But adding these capabilities to an existing architecture may require rebuilding core custody, permission, execution, and fee-routing assumptions.

That is not simply adding a feature.

It is changing the platform model.
